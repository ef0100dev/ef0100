# The 2026-10-29 hot-wallet drain

What ef0100 knows about the drain, where each number comes from, and what is not known. Times are UTC. Tags say how
each statement is supported:

- **[bytes]**: recomputed in this repository from bundled raw bytes.
- **[chain]**: read from chain data through an RPC or an explorer (see [fixtures.md](fixtures.md)).
- **[reported]**: from a public statement or a third party; not checked here.
- **[inferred]**: reasoning from the above.

## In one paragraph

A transaction with 50 EIP-7702 authorizations, all signed by the key of the hot wallet `0x28C6c062…1d60` (explorer
label "Binance 14"), each for a different consecutive nonce and all delegating to `0x63e842…6fAe`, landed in block
26,300,753 at 20:20:59. One authorization matched the wallet's nonce at that moment. From then until 00:10:35 the next
day, the wallet's code was `0xef0100 ‖ 63e842…6fae` and any call to it ran the delegate's code, which swept its ETH and
tokens to two collector addresses. The wallet kept sending its own transactions for about 20 minutes, then a handful,
then an evacuation from 22:55; nothing stopped its key from signing. **[bytes, chain]**

## The delegation transaction **[bytes]**

- `0xad99264d5532ae0dd04b2004eebda05f25657e70dacb764f7ac8d4ccd051298b`, type 4, 4,916 bytes; `keccak256` of the raw
  bytes is the hash.
- Sent by `0x7C8601d4645b93cea45F71e118A729332e6B5CA0` (its nonce 10) to the wallet, with empty calldata and value 0.
- 50 authorizations: chain id 1, delegate `0x63e842B054Bd624a465454D533C33F573e9c6fAe`, nonces 18,914,824 down to
  18,914,775, one apart. All 50 recover to `0x28C6c06298d514Db089934071355E5743bf21d60`.
- Gas used 1,258,562 = 21,000 + 50 × 25,000 − 12,500 (the refund for the one tuple that applied) + 62 (the delegate's
  `receive()`). **[bytes, chain]**

## Why exactly one applied **[bytes, chain]**

Clients process an authorization list in order, skip any tuple whose nonce is not the authority's current nonce, and
bump the nonce after each tuple that applies.

- At the end of block 26,300,752 the wallet's nonce was 18,914,808. Its own transaction with that nonce ran at index
  133 of block 26,300,753, five positions before the delegation transaction (index 138), so the nonce was 18,914,809
  when the list ran.
- Tuples #0–#14 carried nonces above 18,914,809 and were skipped; #15 carried 18,914,809 and applied; #16–#49 carried
  nonces already used.
- The wallet's next own transaction used 18,914,810. No transaction ever used 18,914,809: the authorization did.
- In descending order only one tuple can match, so the list used up a single nonce. In ascending order, each applied
  tuple would have left the nonce the next one needed: 16 tuples would have applied and used 18,914,809–18,914,824.
  **[bytes]** (the site's lab recomputes both orders from the raw bytes)

The lowest signed nonce, 18,914,775, is the one the wallet used at 20:17:47 (block 26,300,737). The range stopped being
usable when the wallet itself reached 18,914,824 in block 26,300,770. **[chain]** When the 50 signatures were produced is
not known.

## What went through the delegation **[chain]**

The operator called the wallet 12 times while it was delegated: five sweeps with value, two calls that made a smart
account the wallet owned (`0xEe7aE85f…4055`) send its USDC, and five sweeps that found nothing.

| UTC | Call | Out |
|---|---|---|
| 20:24:23 | sweep #1, 17 blocks (204 s) after the delegation | 167,464.25 ETH, 1,615,409,498.97 USDT, 62 other tokens |
| 20:41:23 | smart account | 780,000,000 USDC |
| 20:59:47 | smart account | 43,000,000 USDC |
| 21:04:23 | sweep #2 | 1,699.92 ETH, 3,917,639.77 USDT, 22 other tokens |
| 21:44:11 | sweep #3 | 369.83 ETH, 200,397,771.82 USDT, 13 other tokens |
| 22:39:35 | sweep #4 | 128.49 ETH, 122 other tokens (its USDT transfer reverted: Tether had blacklisted the wallet at 22:06:23) |
| 23:36:47–23:54:59 | five empty sweeps | nothing |
| 23:56:47 | final sweep | 2,961.91 ETH |

In total: 172,624.40 ETH and 1,819,724,910.57 USDT from 184 token contracts swept from the wallet, plus 823,000,000
USDC out of the smart account. Sweep #1 left exactly 1 ETH behind, so everything the later sweeps took arrived while
the wallet was delegated: sweeps #2–#4 alone took 2,198.24 ETH and 4,315,411.59 USDT beyond the largest top-up. The two
largest top-ups were swept 72 s and 60 s after they landed: 200,000,000 USDT at 21:42:59 and 2,961.99 ETH at 23:55:47.
None of the senders is verified.

Signals that were visible on chain:
- `eth_getCode(wallet)` returned `0xef0100 63e842…6fae` from block 26,300,753 to 26,301,895.
- The main sweep emitted `SweepItemFailed(address,uint8)` from the wallet's own address. An EOA cannot emit logs.
- A plain ETH transfer into the wallet used 21,062 gas instead of 21,000: the delegate's `receive()` ran.
- The wallet's own transactions fell from as many as 8 per block before the delegation to at most 1 per block for the
  1,142 delegated blocks (93 transactions, 1,049 blocks with none), then rose to 28 per block after the clear.
- The delegation transaction does not appear in Flashbots Mempool Dumpster, which logged 11,060 type-4 transactions
  that day, while all 651 of the wallet's own transactions between 19:27 and 24:00 do. That is strong evidence of
  private order flow, not proof. The earliest public trace of the delegation itself was its block.

## The clear **[bytes, chain]**

`0xafbf94c92a8d749988411669fa14d4f8866d9b4420b4ebd299f28e958dac7209`, block 26,301,896, 00:10:35: the wallet sent itself
a type-4 transaction (nonce 18,914,903) whose authorization delegated to `0x0` at nonce 18,914,904, the transaction
nonce + 1. Gas used 36,800. The delegation had been live for 3h49m36s. `planClearDelegation()` rebuilds this
transaction byte for byte from its own signatures.

## The delegate **[bytes]**

`0x63e842B054Bd624a465454D533C33F573e9c6fAe`, 1,674 bytes of runtime code, codehash `0x7de93915…0e5d`. Read
statically: `sweep(address,address[])`, `callExecute(address,address,uint256,bytes)`, `withdrawToken`,
`withdrawETH`, `authorizedCaller()`; one hardcoded caller (the operator); a 1-ether constant it keeps behind; and
`execute(address,uint256,bytes)` stored as `PUSH4 0x5b0e93fb PUSH1 0xe1 SHL`, a selector that never appears as
`b61d27f6` in the bytecode. An earlier version, `0x19beE294…f2bf` (1,256 bytes, no `callExecute`), was used on the
attacker's own test wallets that afternoon. **[bytes, chain]**

## After the sweeps **[bytes, chain]**

`ef0100 replay --flows` prints the first hop. Collector R1 (`0x5227…2f8c`) received the delegation's sweeps from the
wallet, 28,005.60 ETH and 55,739,800.50 USDT as ordinary signed transfers from a second hot wallet (explorer label
"Binance 16"), and the 823,000,000 USDC from the smart account. Collector R2 (`0xA97b…8d10`) received the later
sweeps. Out of R1, three plain ETH transfers ship as raw transactions and decode in the tests: 195,460 ETH to
`0x8532…43D4` at 20:38:47 (block 26,300,842), 2,000 ETH to the same address at 01:14:11, and 17,260 ETH to
`0x03b0…20ea` at 05:15:47. **[bytes]** Both collectors then sent hundreds of transactions to DEX routers, selling the
swept tokens; the counts are a dated Blockscout snapshot. **[chain]** Tether and Circle blacklisted R1 at 21:01:35 and
21:04:59. **[chain]**

## The other legs **[chain, reported]**

EIP-7702 carried one wallet on one chain, as far as these traces go: only this wallet's delegation status was checked
on other chains, and Arbitrum, Base and Polygon outflows were not traced transaction by transaction. On the chains that
were traced, in the same half hour, ordinary signed transfers moved funds out of other wallets on Bitcoin, the XRP
Ledger, Solana, OP Mainnet, Tron, a second Ethereum wallet and Avalanche. They are listed
with an example transaction each in `docs/data/legs-2026-10-29.json`; their amounts are not recomputed here. The
Solana transfer (`5YvwLFWu…`) is bundled: it does not start with the AdvanceNonce instruction that 1,144 of 1,149
sampled transactions from the same hot wallet start with, and it moves 90.4% of the wallet's SOL. `ef0100 sol
--template` flags both.

## Not known

- How the 50 signatures were produced. The exchange's preliminary statement describes a compromised engineer's laptop
  that reached a service container, and says it found no evidence of a key leak **[reported]**. On-chain data cannot
  tell a stolen key from a signing service that was made to sign.
- Whether the same key signed other authorizations that were never used.
- Attribution. ef0100 takes no position.
